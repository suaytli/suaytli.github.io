“Anti-Social Media”

Anti-social media is a movement defined by many intellectual spheres. These spheres use the lenses of corporate analysis, psychology, data protectionism, sociology, and more. But my focus within this movement is different: I have always yearned for MySpace-style customizable web pages. Thanks to my artsy childhood, I find the idea of developing stylish, personal digital artifacts fascinating.

This is my chance to maintain an irregular blog. I am not the type of person to post daily content on Instagram or LinkedIn, so this serves as a way for me to actually produce "output." Hopefully, this "anti"-social project will keep you updated on my life and maybe even expand your taste palette!

#### What I Have Been Thinking Lately

I feel like the current focus on AI is misleading. The real focus will be on the physical infrastructure, which will highlight data centers more than the cognitive part of the technology itself. We are producing more data than we realize, and as a user and a small node in this network, I don't even know my own value. We need to learn more about how we, as nodes, play into this game.

I also think media consumption is too accelerated, and we are "prosuming" only because we produce data with every step we take. I feel like not everyone understands the immensity of the media we consume and how deeply it affects us as a whole. Maybe the real deal is not just having access to the internet and social media, but understanding how the media is shaping us and how it will eventually numb us completely.

The process of looking at an object or a scenery, seeing the vectors, and understanding the lighting is a real game-changer. When I started vectorizing with the pen tool (to curve shapes into the exact mimicry I wanted to achieve) it led me to look at real-life scenery and understand how to "vectorize" it. It is quite similar with watercolor; you learn how to simplify the vibrant colors you see into basic hues and strokes. Everyone should try to mimic nature!

Developing your body and shaping it is incredibly challenging. This is a long-run process that you cannot speed up. The only benefit (other than achieving your dream body) is that you understand your body and your movements better outside of the gym. You start noticing it when simple actions become challenging, like taking a shower and struggling with basic arm movements, or feeling your muscles tighten when you aren't moving.

Note: Data is not just the Excel spreadsheets we see at work, nor is it just a binary string. Even the single image you post on your story or your Spotify listening streak is a data point, and powerful data visualizers will map your whole life with those few fragments of data they have!
