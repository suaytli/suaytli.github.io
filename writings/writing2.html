“By People, For People”

August 4, 2026 · 3 minute read

I have always been a society person. Not because I am the most benevolent person ever or because I am altruistic, but because I always believed that humanity shall unite for simple yet powerful causes. This writing won't be about how we are living as a society, but the importance of the slogan, "By people, for people!"

As you can see from this website or by talking with me personally, I have always been interested in weird, unrelated stuff. This interest is not because I am "autistic," which most of my friends might argue about, but because of my motivation to discover as much as possible. This is a gluttonous act of mine that I developed after the Vox end-of-year videos and songs similar to "Just Dream It" - DPR LIVE. The feeling of creativity, diversity, enigma, and positive feelings always hyped me. Thus motivating me to study and explore the social sciences!

Even though from childhood I explored these concepts prematurely, I always thought when I grew up I would explore a world full of creativity and vision. These Pollyannaish thoughts of mine were not because of my idiocy, but manifested from how the 2000s and early 2010s were fueled by hope and techno-optimism. Looking back from 2026, I still yearn for the creativity and colors of that era.

This is why I write this piece: we lost our color and connectivity. In our everyday lives, we are more connected than ever. We can reach our connections in a multitude of ways and a multitude of volumes, yet we struggle to create something together in a meaningful way. We should have been using this to connect and multiply, yet we are going the way of solitude and singularity.

To battle with this, I created (or founded, in other technical labels) a department student project named "Jakoben Students' Hub." The project targeted a single feeling: unity without a reason. I already study in a faculty with strong historical bonds, yet I felt that absence in my department due to the lack of graduate relations. The project was simply successful and reached more than 40k people. This number is growing every year since we are actively participating in our department's introduction days, semester events, and other partnerships. This project may fail or become a legacy, which I don't care about (doesn't mean that I want it to succeed, though!), but I care about this project because it's a way to reintroduce the concept of "By people, for people!" In this case, we use it as "By students, for students!"

This trend is also supported by foundations and firms such as the Mozilla Foundation and Firefox (which I use for my browser). Even though this behaviour has turned out to be another buzzword for some for-profit firms, and it sometimes feels like the Mozilla Foundation is also becoming less relevant (from time to time), we shall start embracing this motto.

So, this is why you shall also create your own hub, alone or with others! To spread the whimsy, techno-optimism, human creativity, and most importantly, the feeling of diversity. You wouldn't want your internet to be the new cubicles, right?
